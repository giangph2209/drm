How to read the results. With Ctrl+P and F12, the middle column shows keydown — the page receives the event before the browser can act on it, so preventDefault() can block it and the Print dialog never opens. With PrtSc, the middle column shows keyup only or nothing at all — and even when it is received, the screenshot is already in the clipboard by then. That is the whole difference.
The final proof.
  1. Press PrtSc on this page, then click the "Read current clipboard" button. If the warning line appears, the clipboard overwrite worked — but the screenshot was still taken.
  2. Press Win+Shift+S, drag over a region, then open Paint and paste. The image comes out fine — the page has no idea it happened.
  3. Compare with Ctrl+P: the Print dialog does not open. That is a key the page really can block.
